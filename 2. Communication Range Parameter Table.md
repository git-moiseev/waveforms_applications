# Communication Range Parameter Table

The table gives the maximum free-space detection range for a blind noncoherent energy search, assuming equal transmitter and receiver antenna diameters:

$$D_t=D_r=D.$$

The range is calculated from

$$\boxed{R_{\max}=\frac{\pi D^2}{4\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{P_t\eta_t\eta_rL}{\rho_{\mathrm{acq}}k_{\mathrm B}T_{\mathrm{sys}}}}\left(\frac{t_{\mathrm{int}}}{B}\right)^{1/4}}.$$

Fixed assumptions:

- nominal frequency: $f_0=1420.405751\ \mathrm{MHz}$;
- wavelength: $\lambda\simeq0.211061\ \mathrm{m}$;
- equal aperture efficiencies: $\eta_t=\eta_r=0.6$;
- power-transfer factor: $L=1$;
- normalized acquisition threshold: $\rho_{\mathrm{acq}}=6$;
- detector bandwidth: $B=1\ \mathrm{Hz}$;
- distances are expressed in parsecs.

## Scaling

Because $D_t=D_r=D$, the range scales as

$$R_{\max}\propto D^2P_t^{1/2}T_{\mathrm{sys}}^{-1/2}t_{\mathrm{int}}^{1/4}B^{-1/4}\rho_{\mathrm{acq}}^{-1/2}.$$

> Thus, increasing both antenna diameters by a factor of 10 increases the range by a factor of 100, whereas increasing integration time by a factor of 10000 increases the range only by $10000^{1/4}=10$. 

## Notes

- All antenna diameters $D_t$ and $D_r$ are physical reflector diameters in meters.
- Transmitter power $P_t$ is the total RF power delivered to the transmitting antenna, expressed in kilowatts or megawatts.
- Aperture efficiencies $\eta_t$ and $\eta_r$ account for illumination, spillover, surface accuracy, blockage, and other practical losses. Typical values range from 0.5 to 0.8.
- System temperature $T_{\mathrm{sys}}$ includes receiver noise, sky background, atmospheric emission, ground pickup, and spillover. Typical values are about 30 K for a space radio telescope and about 300 K for an uncooled terrestrial receiver.
- Detector bandwidth $B$ is the noise-equivalent bandwidth of one energy-detector cell. A noncoherent range calculation requires both $B$ and $t_{\mathrm{int}}$.
- Integration time $t_{\mathrm{int}}$ is the total noncoherent averaging time. The minimum detectable power decreases as $t_{\mathrm{int}}^{-1/2}$, so maximum range increases as $t_{\mathrm{int}}^{1/4}$.
- Maximum range decreases as $B^{-1/4}$. Narrower cells improve sensitivity but increase the required frequency resolution and may increase the number of search cells.
- The acquisition threshold $\rho_{\mathrm{acq}}$ is the required excess-power significance relative to the rms radiometric noise fluctuation. Values of about 5–7 are typical for a blind search under Gaussian-noise assumptions. Larger values may be required in the presence of interference, non-Gaussian noise, receiver instability, or additional search dimensions.
- The power-transfer factor $L$ is assumed to be close to unity. Its influence is discussed separately in *Propagation Through a Dispersive and Scintillating Channel*.
- The default frequency is the neutral-hydrogen line at 1420.405751 MHz, corresponding to a wavelength of approximately 0.211061 m.
- The result is reported in parsecs and assumes free-space propagation, correct pointing, stationary Gaussian noise, and a signal remaining within one detector cell.
- The calculated range corresponds to the maximum distance at which the transmitted signal reaches the specified acquisition threshold under the assumed system parameters. It does not account for pointing errors, interference, or additional processing gains beyond the specified noncoherent integration time.
