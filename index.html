Notes

Rmax = π Dt Dr / (4 λ) × √(Pt ηt ηr L / (ρacq kB Tsys)) × (tint / B)1/4

  • All antenna diameters Dt and Dr are physical reflector diameters in meters.
  • Transmitter power Pt is the total RF power delivered to the transmitting antenna, expressed in kilowatts.
  • Aperture efficiencies ηt and ηr account for illumination, spillover, surface accuracy, blockage, and other practical losses. Typical values range from 0.5 to 0.8.
  • System temperature Tsys includes receiver noise, sky background, atmospheric emission, ground pickup, and spillover.
  • Detector bandwidth B is the noise-equivalent bandwidth of one energy-detector cell. A noncoherent range calculation requires both B and tint.
  • Integration time tint is the total noncoherent averaging time. The minimum detectable power decreases as tint−1/2, so maximum range increases as tint1/4.
  • Maximum range decreases as B−1/4. Narrower cells improve sensitivity but increase the required frequency resolution and may increase the number of search cells.
  • The acquisition threshold ρacq is the required excess-power significance relative to the rms radiometric noise fluctuation. Values of about 5–7 are typical for a blind search over approximately one million independent frequency cells under Gaussian-noise assumptions. Larger values may be required in the presence of interference, non-Gaussian noise, receiver instability, or additional search dimensions.
  • The power-transfer factor L is assumed to be close to unity. Its influence is discussed separately in Propagation Through a Dispersive and Scintillating Channel.
  • The default frequency is the neutral-hydrogen line at 1420.405751 MHz, corresponding to a wavelength of approximately 0.211061 m.
  • The result is reported in parsecs and light-years and assumes free-space propagation, correct pointing, stationary Gaussian noise, and a signal remaining within one detector cell.
